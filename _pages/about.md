I am a **Tenure-Track Scientist at the Paul Scherrer Institute (PSI)** in Switzerland, working in the **Transport Mechanisms Group of the Laboratory for Waste Management**.

My research lies at the intersection of **applied mathematics, machine learning, scientific computing, and environmental and materials sciences**. I develop data-driven and computational methods for complex scientific problems, with a particular focus on **scientific machine learning, explainable AI, uncertainty quantification, surrogate modelling, and inverse problems**.

A central theme of my work is **trustworthy data science for scientific applications**: connecting data with domain knowledge and physical models to produce results that are not only accurate and computationally efficient, but also interpretable, uncertainty-aware, and scientifically meaningful.

## Current Research

My current research spans three closely connected areas:

### Explainable and uncertainty-aware machine learning for geoscience

I develop machine-learning and computer-vision methods for extracting quantitative information from geological data, particularly drill-core images and data from deep drilling campaigns.

This work is the focus of my SNSF-funded project **“Characterization of Rocks with Explainable and Uncertainty-Aware Machine Learning.”** The project aims to move beyond predictive accuracy towards machine-learning methods that can explain their predictions and quantify their uncertainty.

[Read about the project on the SNSF Data Portal →](https://data.snf.ch/grants/grant/10006952)

[PSI Research Highlight: Artificial intelligence explores the subsurface →](https://www.psi.ch/de/news/psi-stories/kuenstliche-intelligenz-erkundet-das-erdreich)

### Machine-learning-assisted reactive transport and geochemistry

Reactive transport simulations are essential for understanding the long-term evolution of complex subsurface systems, but they can be computationally demanding.

I investigate **machine-learning surrogate models and scientific machine-learning approaches** to accelerate geochemical and reactive transport calculations. Faster models enable applications such as optimization, uncertainty quantification, sensitivity analysis, and long-term predictions relevant to deep geological repositories for radioactive waste.

### Machine learning for sustainable materials

I also apply machine learning and mathematical optimization to materials research.

In a recent interdisciplinary project at PSI, we combined neural-network surrogate modelling with multi-objective optimization to search for cement formulations that simultaneously reduce **CO₂ emissions** while maintaining desirable mechanical properties.

[PSI Research Highlight: Using AI to develop greener cement →](https://www.psi.ch/de/news/medienmitteilungen/mit-ki-zu-grunem-zement)

## From Inverse Problems to Machine Learning

My scientific path started in **numerical mathematics and inverse problems**. During my PhD in Technical Mathematics, I worked on parameter identification and uncertainty quantification for time-dependent partial differential equations.

Since then, I have applied mathematical modelling, data science, and machine learning across a broad range of scientific and engineering domains — including industrial sensors and semiconductor manufacturing, particle accelerators, aerosol physics, nuclear waste management, geoscience, and sustainable materials.

Despite the diversity of these applications, the underlying questions are often remarkably similar:

**How can we combine data, mathematical models, physical understanding, and machine learning to obtain reliable knowledge from complex systems?**

In 2026, I presented this journey in an invited talk at **Women in Data Science Worldwide in Villach**, entitled *“From Inverse Problems to Machine Learning: A data science journey across scientific domains.”*
